Flagon is a business. We take money for what we make, we intend to keep taking it, and we're not shy about that. A company that can't pay its own bills can't keep its promises, and most of this handbook is a promise. So this page is about how the money works, and more importantly, the rules we put on ourselves about how we're willing to earn it.

The short version: we make money from people who have it and are glad to pay us, and we keep it honest. Everything below is us explaining why that's a strategy and not a sentiment.

## We'd rather price low and earn trust

Given the choice between squeezing another few points of margin out of you and keeping your trust, we take the trust every time. Not because we're generous, but because trust is the harder thing to build and the more valuable thing to have. Margin you can add later. A reputation for gouging people follows you around forever.

So we price with a lot of room in it. We'd rather leave money on the table and have you tell someone else we were fair than extract the maximum and have you feel it. See [the long game](/handbook/the-long-game) for how this connects; the whole point is that being here in ten years is worth more than winning this quarter.

<Callout type="brand" title="The rule">
  When we're unsure whether a price is fair, we round in your favor. A customer
  who feels slightly overcharged remembers it. One who feels well treated tells
  people.
</Callout>

## No bill shock, no dark patterns

There's a whole industry of billing tricks, and we don't use any of them. No surprise charges at the end of the month. No pricing that quietly punishes you for growing, right when you can least afford a nasty surprise. No trials that turn into charges you didn't notice agreeing to. No "call sales to find out the price." No maze to cancel.

If you're going to pay us more, you should be able to see it coming from a mile off, and it should be because you're getting more, not because we found a clever place to hide a meter. Billing that ambushes people is a way of borrowing against their trust, and we don't borrow against the future. Not the code, not the trust.

## We make money from people who are happy to pay

The healthiest kind of revenue comes from customers who'd be a little sad if we disappeared. People who use the thing, get real value from it, and think the price is obviously fair. That's who we build the business around.

It means we don't need to trap anyone. We don't need lock-in, hostage data, or contracts designed so leaving hurts. If someone wants to go, they can go, and our bet is that most won't, because staying is the better deal. A business that only survives because leaving is painful is a business that's already lost the argument on the merits.

## Why this is an advantage, not charity

None of this is us being soft. It's a wager about what actually compounds.

Fair pricing means people recommend us without having to make excuses for us. No bill shock means fewer refunds, fewer angry tickets, and customers who stick around long enough to be worth having. Honesty is cheaper to run than deception: you never have to remember the story you told.

And the moral part and the business part are the same part. The company that treats people fairly when it's expensive is the one people keep choosing when they have options. We'd rather earn a smaller amount we can be proud of, forever, than a larger amount we'd have to hide.

If you ever catch our pricing drifting away from this, that's a bug. Tell us, at [hey@flagon.io](mailto:hey@flagon.io) or in [the open](https://github.com/flagon-io). Consistent with our [values](/handbook/values), we'd rather hear it than pretend it isn't happening.
