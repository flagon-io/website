Flagon is a business. We take money for what we make, we plan to keep taking it, and we're not shy about it. A company that can't pay its bills can't keep its promises, and most of this handbook is a promise. So this is how the money works, and the lines we won't cross to earn it.

## We price low, sometimes below cost

We charge less than we could. Where it makes sense we charge less than a thing costs us to run, on purpose, so the free tier stays genuinely useful and the paid price never feels like a stretch. That reads like charity and isn't. It's a bet that the cheapest fair option in a category is the one people stay with and mention to a friend, and that a name for not gouging people outlasts any margin we skip to earn it.

The commitment is low prices that stay low. The old trick, hook people cheap then raise it once they depend on you, is the exact thing this rules out. We'd rather make less per customer and keep far more of them, glad, than squeeze the ones we have until they resent it. When we genuinely can't tell whether a price is fair, we round in your favor.

## No bill shock, no billing tricks

There's a whole industry of billing tricks, and we use none of them. No surprise end-of-month charges. No pricing that quietly punishes you for growing. No trial that flips to a charge you never noticed agreeing to. No "call sales for the price," no maze to cancel. If you ever pay us more, you'll see it coming, and it'll be because you're getting more, not because we hid a meter somewhere clever.

## Revenue from people glad to pay

The healthy kind of revenue comes from people who'd be a little sad if we vanished: they use the thing, get real value, and think the price is obviously fair. That's who the business is built around, and it means we never have to trap anyone. No lock-in, no hostage data, no contract written so that leaving hurts. If you want to go, you can, and we're betting most won't, because staying is the better deal. A company that only keeps customers because leaving is painful has already lost the argument on the merits.

## Why fair pricing wins

Priced fairly, people recommend us without having to make excuses for us. Free of bill shock, we get fewer refunds, fewer furious tickets, and customers who stick around long enough to be worth having. Honesty is cheaper to run than deception: you never have to remember which story you told. The company that treats people fairly when it's expensive is the one they keep choosing once they have options. That is the whole wager.
