Flagon started with a simple, stubborn conviction: that a software company could be run out in the open, by a small crew who actually care, without any of the usual theatre. This is the story of how it began and what it's meant to become.

## Why Chase started it

Chase Pierce started Flagon because he'd spent enough time inside software companies to know how they tend to go. You watch a good idea get sanded down by process. You watch honest work get wrapped in language nobody believes. You watch the gap between what a company says and what a company does get wider every quarter until the two are barely on speaking terms.

None of that is inevitable. Most of it is just habit, copied from the company before, which copied it from the company before that. At some point you stop asking whether the habits are good and start asking why anyone put up with them.

Flagon is the answer to that question. Not a manifesto about it, an actual company that does the work differently and publishes how.

## What was broken

The thing that bothered Chase most wasn't any single policy. It was the two-facedness. Companies run one story internally and a shinier one for the outside world, and the seam between them is where trust goes to die. Employees learn to read between the lines. Customers learn to discount the marketing. Everyone gets a little more cynical, and the company calls it professionalism.

Alongside that: the assumption that bigger is always better, that speed beats care, that you build in a locked room and unveil the finished thing to applause. We think most of that is backwards. The good version of software gets built in daylight, with the people who use it in the room, at a pace the makers can sustain.

## Open from day one

So Flagon has been run by a public handbook since before there was much to put in it. Not as a growth tactic, though we took the idea from companies who proved it works, but because it forces us to be honest. It's hard to quietly adopt a policy you'd be embarrassed to publish. This document is the operating manual and there is no second, secret one.

<Callout type="brand" title="One version">
  There is no internal Flagon and external Flagon. What you read in this
  handbook is what we actually do. If the two ever drift apart, the handbook is
  the bug, and we fix it.
</Callout>

Building in the open means the roadmap, the reasoning, and the misfires are all readable by anyone. It's a higher bar. It's also the only way we know to stay the company we set out to be instead of slowly becoming the thing we were reacting against. You can read the reasoning in [our values](/handbook/values) and see how it plays out in [how we work](/handbook/how-we-work).

## What kind of company this is

Small on purpose. Opinionated on purpose. Built by a crew of misfits who'd rather make one genuinely good thing than a pile of features nobody asked for. We're early, and we say so plainly, but early is a stage, not an apology. We know what we're building and why it deserves to exist.

We're also playing [the long game](/handbook/the-long-game). Flagon isn't a company set up to be flipped. It's meant to still be here, and still be good, years from now, which changes almost every decision you make in the present. You don't borrow against the future to make a quarter look good, not with the code, not with people's energy, not with the trust of anyone who relies on us.

That's how we got here. If you want to know where we're going, the rest of this handbook is the honest version. And if you'd like to talk, we're on [GitHub](https://github.com/flagon-io), in [Discord](https://discord.gg/dtYQs6rPXN), and at [hey@flagon.io](mailto:hey@flagon.io).
