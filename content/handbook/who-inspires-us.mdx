Flagon didn't spring from nowhere. A handful of companies figured out how to do this well before we existed, and we studied them closely. Here's open credit for what we borrowed, because pretending we invented any of it would be both dishonest and boring.

The important part: we take a thread from each, not a blueprint from any one of them. Copying a single company page for page just gets you a worse version of that company. The job is to pull the right ideas from very different places, leave the parts that don't fit us, and weave something that's actually ours.

## GitLab

GitLab treats its handbook as a product, not a memo. Thousands of pages, versioned in git, edited by anyone, radically transparent about how the company actually runs. They also made market-based, location-aware compensation something you could read and reason about instead of negotiate in the dark.

What we took: the handbook-as-a-product idea, the instinct to write the decision down where everyone can see it, and a comp philosophy that's legible rather than secret.

## Basecamp / 37signals

37signals made the case for the calm company: stay small on purpose, hold strong opinions, ignore the hype cycle, and refuse to accept that work has to be frantic to be serious. "It doesn't have to be crazy at work" is basically a load-bearing sentence around here.

And then there's Shape Up, their approach to building software, which we love and lean on hard. Shaping work to the right altitude before anyone commits, betting on it with a fixed appetite instead of grooming an endless backlog, and working in cycles that either ship or get dropped rather than dragging on forever. It's the antidote to estimate theatre, and its fingerprints are all over [how we plan](/handbook/how-we-plan).

What we took: staying small as a choice rather than a phase, a sustainable pace treated as a feature, opinions over trend-chasing, and Shape Up's habit of fixing the appetite and shaping the work instead of estimating it to death.

## PostHog

PostHog proved a company handbook can have a pulse. Theirs is public, opinionated, funny in places, and written like humans wrote it rather than a compliance department. They publish the awkward parts, they built a compensation calculator so pay isn't a mystery, and they build in public without the usual corporate gloss.

What we took: the conviction that a handbook can have personality and still be serious, that transparency about money beats vague promises, and the nerve to publish before everything is perfect.

## Stripe

Stripe made craft a company value you could feel in the product. Their documentation set a bar the whole industry now measures itself against, and their obsessive care for the developer using the thing showed that attention to detail is a business strategy, not a luxury.

What we took: craft as a real value, documentation treated as part of the product, and love for the developer on the other side of the API.

## Linear

Linear showed that taste scales. Tight focus, opinionated defaults, and a level of polish that makes the obvious choice the right one. They proved you can move fast and still make something that feels considered in every corner.

What we took: opinionated defaults done beautifully, ruthless focus, and the belief that taste is worth defending even when it's slower.

## Valve

Valve ran flat and autonomous when that sounded like heresy: people move to the work that matters, no manager assigning it. We don't copy it wholesale, but the idea that ownership beats hierarchy stuck with us.

What we took: the trust that capable people, given room, will find the important work themselves.

## Standing on shoulders

We're standing on other people's shoulders, and we'd much rather admit it than pretend we invented daylight. Everything above is proof that the way we want to run Flagon is possible, because someone already did the hard part of proving it. Our job is to take what they got right, leave what doesn't fit, and add whatever's ours to add. If you see one of these threads in how we work, that's not an accident, it's gratitude with a footnote. You can see where it all lands in [our values](/handbook/values) and [how we work](/handbook/how-we-work).
